# Fiverr: traffic, ranking, and promotion rules

Load this before designing any "get more traffic / more orders" plan for a live Fiverr listing.
Fiverr does not publish its ranking formula, and its policy pages change — re-verify the policy
items at time of use. Treat the numbers below as directional, not as a published spec.

## What the algorithm actually measures

Two stages, and they answer different questions:

1. **Relevance** — does this gig qualify to appear for the query at all. Read in order:
   **title → search tags → description**. This is the *only* lever a seller has before any
   order history exists.
2. **Performance** — once shown, where it sits. Click-through rate, conversion rate,
   response rate/speed, order-completion rate, reviews plus private buyer feedback.

Consequences that drive the plan:

- A gig with impressions but no clicks gets **deprioritised**. Unconverted traffic is worse
   than no traffic — it teaches the algorithm the gig is a bad match.
- A new gig gets a short initial visibility window (order of 48–72 h) and then performance
   decides. Fiverr publishes **no** ranking timeline; any "N days to rank" figure is a guess.
- First orders beat ranking tactics. Roughly the first five well-delivered orders plus the
   reviews that follow build the satisfaction score that ranking then follows from.

## Editing discipline

- Fiverr's own guidance: allow **3–4 weeks (30 days)** after a keyword change before judging
  its impact.
- **One edit, then a freeze.** "Do not stack edits" means do not edit *repeatedly across the
  measurement window* — it does NOT mean change one field per session. Bundle every field you
  intend to change (title + tags + description) into a **single** editing pass, then leave them
  alone for the full 30 days. A second pass days later triggers a second re-review and resets
  the read for no gain.
- At zero impressions there is no signal to attribute, so splitting fields to "isolate the
  variable" buys nothing — it only costs an extra re-review.
- A significant edit can briefly move the gig to **Under review**; returning to ACTIVE within
  a few hours to 48 h is normal. Confirm it is Active again — this is also the first thing to
  check when impressions stay at zero.
- Judge by the gig's own trend across full 30-day analytics windows, not against a quoted
  benchmark.

## Diagnose by stage, not by volume

Read the gig's own 30-day window (impressions → clicks → orders) and fix the stage that is zero:

| Stage reading | What it means | What to change |
|---|---|---|
| No impressions | Not qualifying for the searches that matter | Title / tags / category relevance; confirm the gig is Active, not Pending Approval |
| Impressions but no clicks | Shown, not compelling | First (thumbnail) image, title phrasing, price anchor |
| Clicks but no orders | Page does not close | Offer structure, package contents, description answering the obvious buyer questions, requirements |

## External traffic is permitted — and rewarded

- Fiverr's help center explicitly allows sharing a link to your own website or portfolio in the
  gig. Sellers who clear the first-order barrier fastest typically bring their own audience.
- Therefore "increase traffic" is two separable problems: raise *relevant* impressions inside
  Fiverr, and bring qualified outsiders in. Do not treat them as one.
- **Do the run-rate arithmetic before investing in ranking work.** One order needs on the order
  of 1,100–10,000 impressions and 33–100 clicks. Take the gig's own 30-day impression count,
  convert it to a monthly run rate, and compare it against that band. If the run rate is an
  order of magnitude or more short, ranking tactics cannot close the gap — the constraint is
  *discovery*, not relevance. Say that plainly instead of prescribing keyword work that cannot
  move the number, and spend the effort on bringing an audience in.
- **Publishing the prevention side free is a discovery channel.** A free installable tool (e.g.
  the audit workflow shipped as an Agent Skill) creates inbound from the ecosystem and doubles
  as evidence for the paid service: the free tier prevents, the paid tier verifies. Judge the
  channel by installs/downloads, not by the listing existing — a directory page with zero
  installs is a prepared channel, not traffic. Mechanics: `hermes-agent-skill-authoring`,
  `references/ecosystem-distribution.md`.

## Hard bans (account-suspension risk)

- **Google Ads is explicitly prohibited** for promoting gigs, the seller page, or any Fiverr
  content. Never route a Fiverr URL through a Google Ads campaign, and never recommend it.
- No off-platform payment, external contracts, or contact details; no directing buyers off-platform.
- No unsolicited DMs promoting your gigs (spam); no fake reviews, no artificial traffic or
  engagement signals.
- Whatever external channel you advertise on must itself permit it — obey that platform's own
  ToS (community self-promotion rules, automation limits). Fiverr states this obligation directly.
- Fiverr also forbids promising specific measurable outcomes in gig copy. Fear-framed or
  guarantee-framed pitches are a moderation risk, not just a taste problem.

## Profile Strength (adjacent, easy to over-invest in)

Profile Strength is a 12-point checklist on the **seller dashboard** (not the gig editor).
Fiverr's help center states an incomplete score does **not** affect impressions or seller
metrics — treat it as credibility polish for a human reading the profile, not as a ranking
lever. Its open items are typically: showcase portfolio, intro video, certifications, education.
The LinkedIn connection offered inside it is gated to Fiverr Pro / Top Rated sellers and will
not appear for a new seller.
