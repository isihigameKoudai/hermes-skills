# Service Business Launch

## When to Use
Launching or planning a solo service / freelance business (code review, consulting, audit, design, refactoring). Trigger when the user is defining an offer, choosing distribution channels, pricing, or sequencing "build vs sell" — especially when they push back on building assets before demand exists.

## Order of operations: validate demand before building
A service business validates demand by LISTING the offer, not by building assets first. The sequence:

1. **需要検証** — post the offer where the buyers are (marketplace gig, community) and see if anyone bites. Zero-build, near-zero cost.
2. **最小クローズ** — only when someone bites, build the minimum to close that one deal (contract template + delivery flow, a few hours).
3. **再計算** — overwrite the funnel/pricing model with the measured numbers; change price/structure if it no longer holds, BEFORE scaling.
4. **本格投資** — LP, content engine, OSS, retainer — only after the model holds on real data.

Pitfall: building contracts, portfolio, LP, or OSS BEFORE any demand signal maximizes rework. The offer itself is the validation instrument; "守り(構築)→検証" is the wrong order even when the build feels cheap.

## Verify before asserting — label every claim
Tag market/channel claims `[検証済]` (verified via primary source) vs `[推定]` (inference). Never state a comparative claim ("the buyers are in X, not Y") as fact without checking BOTH sides. A claim that came from the client's own earlier research is not your verification — it is inherited, so re-check it or label it as such. When challenged for evidence, answer by separating what you directly verified from what was inherited vs what is inference — do not overstate.

## Check a channel's current rules before recommending it
Community self-promotion rules change. Before recommending "post your offer in <community>", check its CURRENT rules: a "no <topic> pessimism" ban or a "tools need mod approval" rule can turn a promotional post into a permanent ban. When a community is hostile to negative framing, reframe the content as an empowering educational checklist, and route the direct offer to a platform with no such rule.

## Check a marketplace's hard constraints before committing
- Minimum price floor (some marketplaces reject offers below a floor — check before pricing the entry-tier offer).
- Category is often locked after publish; choose it correctly the first time.
- Commission and payout terms reduce the net — compute net revenue, not gross.

## A platform is one channel, not the market
A marketplace's own landing-page positioning (creative-first vs dev-first) is a signal, but verify demand on BOTH the platform and the community before ranking channels. For a service, the cheapest and most accurate demand signal is usually the community where the buyer already asks for the exact help — check both sides before declaring where the buyers live.
